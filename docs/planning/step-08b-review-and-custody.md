# Step 08 human review, multilingual coverage and independent custody

2026-10-25. Specification only; no reviewer was hired/contacted, packet issued, decision entered, protected case obtained or source accessed. Companion: [corpus readiness](step-08b-corpus-readiness.md). Existing acquisition is finished; this work concerns the scientific validity of the corpus.

## 1. Review responsibilities and stages

A **review packet** is a fixed collection of original evidence and questions. An **adjudication** is a documented resolution by an authorized competent human, including permission to leave a case unresolved. Neither software nor an LLM is a human reviewer.

| Role | Responsibility | Required before execution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project owner | Population/type choice, scope, reviewer time/payment and approvals | Named accountable owner; approved review protocol, budget and retention |
| Corpus curator | Source provenance, packet preparation, identity evidence requests, exposure log | Authorized access; cannot silently collect additional fields |
| First and second reviewer | Independent initial judgments of names, identity evidence and relation/direction | Verified human identity/authorization, language/domain competence, availability and conflict declaration |
| Adjudicator | Resolve disagreements, weak-name purpose exceptions, used hard negatives and graph contradictions | Appropriate competence; separate from initial reviewers for disputed/final decisions under the proposed protocol |
| Independent custodian/evaluator | Control unexposed material, final review access and generic integrity outputs | Separate account/environment unavailable to developers and this unrestricted agent; access evidence |
| Codex | Prepare/validate artifacts, apply recorded human decisions mechanically, report unresolved gates | No self-created reviewer identity, human attestation, translated label or approval |

Recommended sequence after software implementation and owner approval: calibrate instructions on fictional cases; then a **40-case development pilot** (20 positive proposals, 20 negative candidates); independently review and adjudicate; measure time, evidence sufficiency and disagreement; freeze/revise the protocol; expand to at least 200+200 distinct reviewed cases. The 40 may count toward 400 only if the frozen protocol remains applicable and each has complete retained evidence/submissions; protocol changes require explicit re-review, not fabricated backdating. No final cases belong in reviewer training or this pilot.

## 2. Minimum 200 positive and 200 negative candidate reviews

This is **400 distinct inspected candidate cases**, not a guarantee of 400 accepted labels or a certificate for the whole 7,000-family corpus. Two directions of one pair, repeated submissions or repeated aliases from one case do not inflate the minimum. Prefer different underlying families within each sampling cell and report family reuse explicitly. Adequate eligibility/identity review is still needed for every counted corpus family and every label used. Provider assertions can remain unreviewed evidence; weak supervision is a separate policy requiring approval and separate reporting.

These are proposed case allocations, not selected cases or verified feasible resolved-label counts. Negative strata refer to the query's evidence context, not an automatic different-identity label. Ensure each of the three sources appears as an endpoint in at least 20 cases overall, with explicit within/cross-source counts; balance using a deterministic hash-sorted sampling rule and fixed tie-breaks before review. Source minima are marginals, not additional cases. Duplicate/quarantined cases may be reviewed to resolve them, but cannot yield usable labels until their blockers are resolved. Include weak/ambiguous cases as diagnostic items; count them as reviewed even when rejected, never as accepted positives/negatives.

If a stratum lacks distinct qualified cases or competent reviewers, record its shortfall and ask for a revised sampling/evidence package. Negative candidates may be nominated by confusion/equality rules or a later authorized training-only miner. Hide nomination scores/ranks and proposed answers from reviewers. Equality candidates frequently turn out to be same or unresolved; the result determines eligibility, not the queue name. Every hard negative actually used, including later mined/in-batch negatives, needs explicit supported-distinct adjudication and eligible different families. No low score, unclicked candidate, different source ID or missing link proves distinction.

## 3. Required packet and decision fields

Two distinct schemas are needed: (a) a **readiness preparation manifest**, bound to the continuation receipt/selections and carrying unreviewed nominations; (b) a later **real review packet**, bound to an approved candidate artifact and authorized human workflow. Do not reuse `candidate_release_hash` to mean an acquisition receipt hash. Current `label_review.py` accepts fictional packets only; never bypass that gate or set a real packet's mode to fictional.

| Object | Required fields |
| --- | --- |
| Preparation/packet header | Explicit kind/schema version; content hash; parent input-manifest/release hash; packet ID; source snapshot/receipt/grant refs; purpose, development/protected custody mode; frozen sampling rule/version and quotas; nomination method/version; protocol version; creator/time; approval; maximum fields/size; human decision count initially zero |
| Case | Stable content-bound case ID; both observation/source-qualified IDs and snapshots; original name-evidence IDs and text; primary/alias/historic/weak qualifiers; source field paths; provider language separately from reviewer annotation; detected/mixed/unknown scripts; entity type evidence; relationship question and required competencies; evidence references; relevant unresolved component/quarantine flags; prior exposure/custody status |
| Permitted identity support | Exact official/provider evidence reference and content hash, field scope and permission; source-provided explicit cross-reference if available; retrieval date/revision. A blank or insufficient support field stays insufficient. Never invent registration evidence or expand retained personal/address fields without approval. |
| Relationship annotation | Translation, transliteration, spelling variation, abbreviation, historic-name change, other supported relation, or unresolved; direction-specific query/reference adequacy; what identity/record-target definition is supported |
| Submission | Human actor/authorization/competency refs; immutable packet/case hashes; UTC timestamp; outcome; rationale citing evidence; uncertainty and conflict flags; scope of identity claim; duration; origin `human`; independent first-pass status |
| Adjudication | Bound case and initial submissions; authorized adjudicator; outcome/reasons/evidence; disagreement retained; purpose-specific weak-name exceptions if any; label-use permissions; superseded decision reference and reason; graph/absence impacts |
| Acceptance/export record | Evidence-backed target set or supported-distinct relation; family/graph/version; quarantine clearance; exact allowed purpose; query/view binding when applicable; human provenance; exposure restriction; no model-training eligibility inferred from review alone |

Reviewers see original evidence in an inert display: source text is untrusted data, escaped as text, never instructions, executable markup or shell content. Reviewer-facing questions should be neutral; keep sampling category/proposed label and model score in an access-separated audit manifest where practical. Log evidence insufficiency, not a guessed answer. Do not send real names to external LLMs or unapproved translation services.

## 4. Decision and adjudication rules

- Permitted case outcomes: `supported_same`, `supported_distinct`, `insufficient_evidence`, `ineligible`. Define “same” as the declared research record/identity target, not wrongdoing. A shared source record may support its publisher's alias assertion; the reviewer must separately assess linguistic relation and ambiguity. Cross-source legal identity often requires more than names.
- Two initial reviewers work independently without seeing one another's answers. Record raw first-pass agreement/disagreement before adjudication. Agreements still undergo eligibility and scope validation; the label is not accepted merely because two people clicked the same button.
- A qualified adjudicator resolves discrepancies, contradictory graph paths and any insufficient evidence later supplemented. Final cases and disputed/weak cases require the independent process proposed here. If staffing cannot support it, stop and request an explicit protocol decision; never claim double review from two AI agents or two accounts belonging to one person. Original review/custody requirements cannot be silently reduced.
- Used hard negatives require positive evidence of distinction plus no accepted same/dependent family and no unresolved link. Parent/subsidiary or shared-brand relation may be distinct legal entities yet dependent examples; study-specific negative eligibility must be explicit. All valid targets in an ambiguous multi-target query must be retained, not converted into negatives.
- Review counts distinguish prepared cases, actual human-reviewed distinct cases, submissions, family coverage, resolved/adjudicated cases, accepted labels and unresolved/excluded cases. Report uncertainty, exclusion, evidence-request and disagreement rates with denominators. A balanced 200/200 queue does not estimate real-world prevalence or model precision.
- Corrections are append-only events with supersession. Reject duplicate/replayed/stale submissions, unauthorized actors, missing evidence, fabricated timestamps, wrong packet hashes and changing originals. A registry entry documents authorization but does not itself authenticate a person; owner-verified identities and an actual access process are necessary.

## 7. Protected final examples and labels

The current workspace is developer-accessible; Unix private modes, another folder, a hash, or encryption with a developer-readable key do not establish independent custody. We have not created or verified a protected storage boundary. A named owner decision and actual separate access arrangement are required before new material can be called protected.

Proposed process:

1. Name a custodian with no model-tuning role, independent language reviewers and evaluator. Use a separately administered account/device/storage environment not mounted here or accessible to this agent's tools, shell, connectors, backups or credentials. Owner confirms actual roles and access, not merely JSON actor labels.
2. Before protected acquisition, transfer the permitted development exposure inventory to the custodian through an approved route. It includes all historical public subsets, current 13,658 observations, aliases/derivatives and known cross-source equivalence/dependence history. The custodian checks freshness privately across source copies, renamed IDs and related evidence. A new source, timestamp or alias does not make an old family fresh; unresolved exposure overlap is excluded/quarantined.
3. Necessary curation/review access is logged separately from development exposure. Reviewers of final cases must not tune models or reveal cases, difficult examples or scores to developers. Keep protected family identities, packet fields, labels, directions and fine-grained selection manifests in custody. Store calibration-fit and threshold-selection pools separately with purpose-limited roles; exposed current families do not become fresh calibration/threshold data by relabeling.
4. Developers build generic code against fictional fixtures and exposed development material. The custodian runs the frozen hash-bound code privately with outbound network disabled for evaluation. Use scoped credentials, access logs, encrypted backups and a verified restore/access policy; encryption is one control, not proof of independence.
5. Before Step 28, developer outputs are only predeclared coarse eligibility counts, whole-artifact commitments and generic integrity pass/fail. No names, provider IDs, per-case hashes, packet contents, rankings, scores, errors, slice metrics or adaptive model-performance feedback. Rare slices must not leak identities by differencing repeated aggregate queries. Custodian receives detailed failures and supplies a generic bug class for a fictional reproducer.
6. Step 09 later freezes whole-family partitions and direction-specific views under separate approval. Final families do not appear in developers' indexes as distractors, mining pools, review memory, training pairs or debug caches. The evaluator's final reference view and any role-specific index differences are explicitly bound and reported.
7. Freeze model/code/config/thresholds, evaluation protocol, denominators and authorized run count before confirmation. Custodian releases final results only through the later approved confirmation procedure; no cherry-picked reruns. If any final material reaches development, log the incident, invalidate affected confirmation eligibility and obtain a new independent cohort/version. Never erase the exposure record or silently resplit the same cases.

Final acceptance requires evidence of the actual access boundary, reviewer independence, protected-input acquisition, complete family exclusion/lineage and custodian integrity checks. Codex can implement checks and inspect non-sensitive attestations, but cannot independently guarantee what humans have seen or make this workspace secret from itself.
